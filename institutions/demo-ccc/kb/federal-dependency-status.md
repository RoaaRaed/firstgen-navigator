# Dependency Status

Your dependency status determines whose information you must report when you fill out the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA®) form.

If you’re a dependent student, you’ll report both your and your parents’ information.

If you’re an independent student, you’ll report your own information (and, if you’re married, your spouse’s).

The FAFSA form collects information from you and your parent(s) to create a full picture of your family’s resources. This enables us to determine your eligibility for federal student aid. A parent who adds information to your FAFSA form isn't required to pay for your education.

## Dependent or Independent

Your answers to personal circumstance questions on the FAFSA® form determine whether you are considered a dependent or independent student.

Here are the questions that determine your dependency status for the 2027–28 school year.

- Were you born before Jan. 1, 2004?
- As of today, are you married? (Answer “No” if you are separated but not divorced.)
- At the beginning of the 2027–28 school year, will you be enrolled in a master’s or doctorate program (such as an M.A., MBA, M.D., J.D., Ph.D., Ed.D., graduate certificate, etc.)?
- Are you currently serving on active duty in the U.S. armed forces for purposes other than training? (If you are a National Guard or Reserves enlistee, are you on active duty for other than state or training purposes?)
- Are you a veteran of the U.S. armed forces?*
- Do you have children or other people (excluding your spouse) who live with you and who receive more than half of their support from you now and between July 1, 2027, and June 30, 2028?
- At any time since you turned 13, were you an orphan (no living biological or adoptive parent)?
- At any time since you turned 13, were you a ward of the court?
- At any time since you turned 13, were you in foster care?
- Are you or were you a legally emancipated minor, as determined by a court in your state of residence?
- Are you or were you in a legal guardianship with someone other than your parent or stepparent, as determined by a court in your state of residence?
- At any point in 2026 or later, were you not living with your parent or guardian, and were you either (1) homeless or (2) self-supporting and at risk of being homeless?

### Answer “Yes” (you are a veteran) if you meet both conditions

You served

- on active duty in the U.S. armed forces (military, naval, air, or space service); or
- as a reservist or member of the National Guard and were called up to active federal duty by presidential order for a purpose other than training; or
- on active or inactive duty for training in the U.S. armed forces and were disabled due to injury received or made worse in the line of duty.

You didn’t receive a dishonorable discharge when you were released.

Also answer “Yes” if you aren’t a veteran now but will be one by June 30, 2028.

### Answer “No” (you are not a veteran) if you

- have never engaged in active duty in the U.S. armed forces;
- are currently a Reserve Officers’ Training Corps (ROTC) student, a cadet, or midshipman at a service academy;
- are currently serving in the U.S. armed forces and will continue to serve through June 30, 2028; or
- were engaged in active duty in the U.S. armed forces but released under dishonorable conditions.

### Homeless or at risk of being homeless

You’ll be able to fill out the FAFSA form as an independent student if you respond to this question that you aren’t living with your parent or guardian, and you’re homeless or at risk of being homeless. For more guidance, see our help article about answering the "Student Homelessness" question.

## Answered “Yes” to One or More of the Questions Above

If you answered “yes” to any of the questions, you’re considered an independent student for federal student aid purposes. Your parents won’t be required to add their information to your FAFSA form.

## Answered “No” to Every Question

If you answered “no” to every question, you’re considered a dependent student for federal student aid purposes. Your parents must provide their information on your FAFSA form.

For the purposes of federal student aid, the following reasons alone won't make you an independent student:

- You live apart from your parents.
- Your parents don't claim you as a dependent on their tax return.

## Which Parent(s) Should Add Their Information to My FAFSA Form

Use our tool for identifying which parent(s) should provide information on the FAFSA form.

## I Don’t Live With My Parents

If you’re a dependent student and don’t live with your parents, you still have to provide parent information.

## My Parents Refuse To Provide Information

If your parents refuse to add their information to your FAFSA form, it doesn't make you an independent student, and you won't be eligible for a Federal Pell Grant or other types of federal student aid. You may be eligible only for a limited amount of a Direct Unsubsidized Loan. Learn what it means to apply only for a Direct Unsubsidized Loan.

## I Don’t Have Contact With My Parents

If you don’t have contact with your parents, you may have an unusual circumstance (e.g., you left home due to an abusive environment or you are a victim of human trafficking). In such situations, you may be considered a provisionally independent student. Learn more about unusual circumstances and how to fill out the FAFSA form if you have one.

## Changes to My Background Information

If your background information changes after you submit your FAFSA form, contact your college’s or career school’s financial aid office to ask how to update your dependency status. The financial aid staff will tell you what to do.
